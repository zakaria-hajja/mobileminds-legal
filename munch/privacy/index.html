Munch Privacy Policy

Effective date: 15 November 2026

This policy explains what happens to your data when you use Munch: Subscription Creatures ("Munch"), our subscription tracker for Android and iPhone. Munch is published by Hajja Labs, the trading name of Zakaria Hajja ("we", "us"), who is the data controller for the data described here.

The short version

  • Your subscriptions stay on your phone. The names, prices and dates you enter are stored only on your device. Munch has no account, no login and no server of its own, and never sends them to us.
  • Munch shows one small ad (a banner on the Home screen), served by Google AdMob. Google receives device and usage data to show and measure that ad.
  • We use Google Firebase to count how the app is used and to receive crash reports. These never include the names, prices or dates of your subscriptions.
  • Where the law requires it, Munch asks for your consent first, and nothing is sent to Firebase for analytics or crash reports until you have answered. Your answer decides whether identifiers are stored for analytics and whether ads can be personalised; crash reports and anonymous usage counts are sent either way (see section 4). On iPhone, Munch asks for permission before tracking.
  • Reminders are created on your phone. They are not sent from a server.
  • Munch is not meant for children.

1. Data that stays on your device

Everything you type into Munch stays in the app's private storage on your phone:

  • your subscriptions ("creatures"): name, price, currency, billing cycle, category and colour, renewal and free-trial dates, reminder settings, and its feeding and release history (including the saving);
  • your settings (currency, reminder days and time), your points, badges and ad-free time;
  • small app records such as how many times the app has been opened (used, for example, to show no ad on your first launch).

Munch does not send this data to us or to anyone else, and we cannot see it. We do not ask for your name, email address, bank details or any login.

Phone backups

Munch has no cloud backup of its own, but your phone's own backup may include Munch's data. How it comes back depends on your phone:

  • Android: if backup to your Google account is on, Android stores Munch's data in that account. It comes back when you reinstall Munch while signed in to the same Google account, or when you set up a new phone from that backup.
  • iPhone: an iCloud or computer backup of your iPhone includes Munch's data, but it comes back only when you restore the whole iPhone (or set up a new iPhone) from that backup. Deleting Munch deletes its data; reinstalling it from the App Store starts empty.

These backups are run by Google or Apple under your account and their privacy terms, not by us. We have no access to them. You can turn backups off in your phone's settings.

Reminders

Renewal and trial reminders are scheduled by Munch on your phone as local notifications. No reminder data is sent to a server. Munch asks for permission to send notifications and works without it.

2. Data that leaves your device

Munch itself sends nothing anywhere. Data leaves your device only through the Google services built into the app, described below. All of it is sent encrypted (HTTPS).

2.1 Advertising: Google AdMob

Munch shows one banner ad on the Home screen. It never shows ads on your first launch, never in Settings, and none while you have ad-free time. The ads are provided by Google AdMob (Google LLC / Google Ireland Ltd.). To choose, show and measure the ad, and to prevent fraud, the Google Mobile Ads SDK in the app collects and shares with Google:

  • device identifiers: the advertising ID on Android (and the app set ID), and on iPhone the advertising identifier (IDFA) only if you allow tracking;
  • approximate location, worked out from your IP address (not GPS);
  • ad interactions: which ads were shown and whether you tapped them;
  • diagnostics about how the ad SDK performs (for example, load times and errors).

Google uses this data under its own terms. See How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services and the Google Privacy Policy. Whether the ads are personalised depends on your consent and your device settings (see section 4).

2.2 Usage analytics: Google Analytics for Firebase

We use Google Analytics for Firebase to understand how Munch is used as a whole: for example, how many people add a subscription, feed or release a creature, or allow notifications. It records:

  • which screens are opened, and events such as "creature added", "creature fed", "creature released", "reminder opened", "ad-free time redeemed" and "notification permission answered";
  • small details about those events, for example: for a subscription picked from Munch's built-in list, which listed service it was (for example "netflix"), or "custom" if you typed your own; whether it is a free trial; a creature's growth stage; points earned; the number of ad-free days redeemed; whether you switched the total to monthly or yearly; which action you undid; which kind of reminder you opened; and your answer to the notification permission;
  • if you consent, or where consent is not required: an app instance ID (a random ID for this installation of the app) stored on your device for analytics. If you decline, events are still sent, but without this ID and without storing or reading an analytics identifier on your device, so they cannot be connected to each other;
  • a Firebase installation ID (a random ID for this installation of the app), and general device information that Firebase collects automatically (such as device model, operating system version, app version, language and an approximate country or region derived from your IP address).

Analytics never include the name you typed for a subscription, its price or its dates. We do not use Analytics to identify you, and we do not link it to advertising.

2.3 Crash reports: Firebase Crashlytics

If Munch crashes or hits an error, Firebase Crashlytics sends us a crash report: the technical error and where in the code it happened, the device model, operating system and app version, and a Firebase installation ID. We use it only to find and fix bugs. Crash reports do not include your subscriptions. Crash reports are sent once you have answered the consent form (or straight away where no consent form is needed), whether you accepted or declined.

2.4 Remote settings: Firebase Remote Config

Munch may download settings from Firebase Remote Config (for example, the list of popular services you can pick from) so we can adjust it without an app update. To do this, it sends a Firebase installation ID and basic app and device information. This happens each time the app starts, including before you answer the consent form; it is not used for analytics or advertising.

Firebase is provided by Google and processes this data on our behalf. See Privacy and Security in Firebase.

2.5 Consent form: Google User Messaging Platform

To ask for your consent where the law requires it (section 4), Munch uses Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP), which is part of Google's ad services. Each time Munch starts, wherever you are, UMP contacts Google to check whether you need to see the consent form, and shows it if you do. For this, Google receives:

  • approximate location, worked out from your IP address (not GPS), to tell whether consent rules apply where you are;
  • your interactions with the form: that it was shown and the choices you made;
  • diagnostics about how the form loads and performs (for example, load times and errors).

Your choices are saved on your phone, in the standard format ad services use (the IAB Transparency and Consent Framework), so that Munch and the Google Mobile Ads SDK can follow them and the form does not appear on every start. We use UMP only to ask for, record and apply your choices. You can change them at any time in Settings > Privacy choices.

4. Your choices

  • Consent (EEA, UK, Switzerland and other places where the law requires it). Munch shows Google's consent form before any analytics, crash reporting or ads data is collected. Until you have answered, nothing is collected for analytics or crash reporting, and no ad is requested. Once you have answered, whether you accept or decline, crash reports (section 2.3) and the usage events in section 2.2 are sent to Firebase. If you decline, no analytics identifier is stored on or read from your device, so the events cannot be connected to each other or to you, and Analytics is never used for advertising. Google may still show non-personalised or limited ads. You can change or withdraw your consent at any time in Settings > Privacy choices.
  • Objecting to crash reports and usage counts. We rely on our legitimate interests for these (section 3). You have the right to object: email us (section 9) and we will look at your request.
  • Tracking on iPhone. Before the first ad loads, Munch asks for permission to track you under Apple's App Tracking Transparency. If you say no, the advertising identifier (IDFA) is not shared. You can change this in iPhone Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking.
  • Advertising ID on Android. You can reset or delete your advertising ID in Android Settings (Privacy > Ads, or Google > Ads, depending on your phone).
  • Notifications. You can turn reminders off in your phone's notification settings at any time.
  • Ad-free time. Points you earn in Munch can be spent on time without ads.

5. Who receives data

We do not sell your personal data for money, and we do not share your subscriptions with anyone. The only recipient of the data in section 2 is Google, as the provider of AdMob and its consent form (UMP), which use data under Google's own terms, as an independent controller for some purposes, and of Firebase (which acts as our processor). Google may process data in the United States and other countries, under safeguards such as the EU Standard Contractual Clauses and the EU–US Data Privacy Framework.

We may disclose data if the law requires it. If Munch is transferred to another company, this policy continues to apply to data collected under it.

6. How long data is kept

  • On your device: until you delete it, clear the app's data or uninstall the app.
  • Firebase Analytics: user-level analytics data is kept for no longer than 14 months, then deleted. Aggregated reports (with no ID) may be kept longer.
  • Crashlytics: crash reports are kept for a limited time (about 90 days) under Google's Crashlytics retention rules.
  • Your consent choices: on your phone until you change them, clear the app's data or uninstall the app.
  • AdMob and the consent form: Google keeps ad data according to its own retention policies, described in the Google Privacy Policy.

7. Deleting your data

  • One subscription: open the creature in Munch and delete it.
  • Everything Munch stores: uninstall the app. On Android you can also clear its data in Settings > Apps > Munch > Storage > Clear storage. Remember that a phone backup may still hold a copy until that backup is replaced or deleted.
  • Analytics and crash data: we cannot link this data to you, because we hold no name, email or account, and the app does not show its analytics identifiers. Email us (section 9) and we will tell you what we can do. Analytics data is deleted automatically after the period in section 6.

8. Children

Munch is a tool for adults managing their own subscriptions. It is not directed to children, and we do not knowingly collect data from children under 13 (or under the age of digital consent where you live, such as 16 in some EU countries). If you believe a child has used Munch and data about them was collected through the services in section 2, contact us and we will help remove it.

9. Your rights and how to contact us

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR in the EU, the UK GDPR, or US state laws such as the California Consumer Privacy Act), you may have the right to access, correct, delete or receive a copy of your personal data, to object to or restrict its use, to withdraw consent, and to opt out of the "sale" or "sharing" of personal data for targeted advertising. We will not treat you differently for using these rights.

Because Munch keeps your subscriptions only on your device, you already have full control over them. For anything else, write to mblminds@gmail.com. We will answer within one month (45 days for requests under US state laws). You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

To opt out of personalised ads (including, in the US, the "sale" or "sharing" of personal data for targeted advertising), use your device settings: reset or delete your advertising ID on Android, or turn off tracking for Munch on iPhone (section 4). Where Munch shows Settings > Privacy choices, you can also change your ad choices there. Email is for questions and data rights requests; we cannot opt a device out of Google's ads for you.

10. Security

Munch's data stays in the app's private storage on your phone, which other apps cannot read. Data sent by the Google services goes over encrypted connections. No method of storage or transmission is perfectly secure, so keep your phone locked and up to date.

11. Changes to this policy

If Munch starts handling data differently, we will update this page and its effective date before the change reaches the app. For important changes, such as a new type of data leaving your device, we will also tell you in the app or in the store listing. Earlier versions are available on request.
